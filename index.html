About Us

HKS Dance is the next chapter of Azad Gharibian Dance (AGD) — a not-for-profit organisation formed in Melbourne on March 2nd, 1994, dedicated to promoting and preserving the ancient art of Armenian dance and culture in Australia. AGD is the first and only Armenian dance group in Victoria.

With over 80 students across Junior, Intermediate, Senior and Social groups — ages ranging from four to 70+ — we pride ourselves on our inclusive culture and accessibility to all ages and abilities, creating a place for Armenians to honour their traditions and express creativity and passion.

Since 1994, AGD has performed all across Australia at multicultural events, festivals, weddings and concerts, often collaborating with other Armenian and multicultural dance groups, celebrating the rich culture and diversity within Australia.

Our Legacy — Azad Gharibian

Azad Gharibian Dance is named after and is a tribute to the late Azad Gharibian, a famous choreographer and dancer from Armenia. Azad dedicated his life travelling around Armenia to learn the depths of his culture and heritage, collecting and re-creating folkloric dances from different regions of the country. He visited villages, observed their traditional dances and nuanced rituals, and breathed new life into Armenian dances — some centuries old — that would have otherwise been forgotten.

'Krounk' (translating to crane) was Azad's favourite bird and symbolic to the Armenian people. Cranes never travel alone, always in groups — a trait Azad connected with deeply. In the 1980s, he formed the 'Krounk Dance Ensemble'. After his sudden passing in 1988, his students vowed to keep his legacy alive — one of them being our founder, Hermine Kuyumcian.

Our Founder — Hermine Kuyumcian

Born in Yerevan, Armenia, Hermine started her dancing career at the age of five. By 12, she became an assistant dance teacher for the "Yeridasartootyun" Dance Group — despite being the youngest in her group. At 16, she was invited to join the "Krounk" dance group as a professional dancer under the renowned Azad Gharibian, travelling the world to perform and constantly inspired by his work.

After Azad's sudden passing in 1988, Hermine vowed to honour his name, memory and choreography. In 1994, she formed Azad Gharibian Dance Group — a living tribute to his enduring legacy. For over 30 years, Hermine has taught Armenian dance in Australia, inspiring generations of dancers to keep their rich culture and heritage alive.

"Words cannot express how proud I am of all my students — from past to present, juniors to seniors — for everything they've achieved throughout the years, and importantly, for keeping our rich culture and heritage alive in Australia."
— Hermine Kuyumcian
